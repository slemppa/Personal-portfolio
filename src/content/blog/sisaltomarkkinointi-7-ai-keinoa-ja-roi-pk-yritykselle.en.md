## The risks of AI — and how to avoid them

AI in marketing isn’t only good. There are also risks that nobody talks about enough. Here are some of the most common ones and how to avoid them.

### Hallucinations: When AI lies

AI can produce content that sounds good but is completely wrong. This is called hallucinations. For example, AI might claim a product does something it doesn’t actually do. This can lead to customer complaints or even legal action.

How to avoid it: Always have a human review AI-generated content. Never publish anything without quality assurance.

### Brand voice fragmentation: When AI sounds like a robot

AI can produce content that sounds generic and impersonal. This can damage your brand if your customers expect personal and human communication.

How to avoid it: Give the AI clear guidance on your brand voice. Use examples and train the AI to recognize the right tone. And remember: AI is a tool, not a replacement.

### Data protection: When AI leaks data

AI tools often process sensitive data, such as customer information. If a tool is not GDPR-compliant, you can run into big problems.

How to avoid it: Choose only GDPR-compliant tools. Make sure data is encrypted and stored in the EU. And remember: never feed sensitive data into AI without protections.

### Copyright: Who owns AI-generated content

AI can produce content that is based on existing material. This can lead to copyright infringements if you’re not careful.

How to avoid it: Use only AI that is trained on your own data. Make sure the AI does not copy existing content. And remember: always human quality assurance.

### Human quality assurance: Why you can’t rely on AI alone

AI is a good tool, but it doesn’t replace a human. If you rely on AI alone, you may publish incorrect or irrelevant content.

How to avoid it: Always have a human check AI-generated content. Make sure the content is correct, relevant, and aligned with your brand.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which content marketing tasks is AI best suited for in an SMB?

AI is best suited for repetitive tasks: clustering content ideas, blog outlines, social and newsletter versions, updating old content, and reporting. It doesn’t replace customer understanding or strategy, but it significantly speeds up the manual work that otherwise quietly eats away a marketing lead’s week.

### How much time and money can AI realistically save in content production?

A realistic saving in an SMB is often 3–10 hours a week, if AI is connected to a clear process and not just random text generation. If the value of internal work is €50 per hour, an 8-hour weekly saving means roughly €1,600 in monthly value before tool costs.

### Which KPI metrics should you track for AI’s benefits in content marketing?

Track at least production speed, time spent editing, organic impressions, CTA click-through rate, internal clicks, cost per lead, and conversion rate. A good additional metric is content update efficiency: how many old pieces of content were improved, and did their rankings or clicks improve.

### What risks are associated with using AI in content marketing?

The biggest risks are invented facts, a generic brand voice, slipping data protection, copyright issues, and humans stopping their thinking too early. Use AI as an assistant, but keep quality assurance with a human. Otherwise, you’ll publish content faster that doesn’t necessarily help sales.

### Should an SMB buy AI tools or outsource implementation to a partner?

If the need is light, start with your own tool and one clear use case. If you want to connect AI to CRM, newsletters, social media, and reporting, a partner can save you from mistakes and speed up payback. A good rule of thumb: buy the tool for experimentation, a partner for building the process.
